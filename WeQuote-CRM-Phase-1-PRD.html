Section 1Phase 1 committed

Executive decision

WeQuote CRM separates two kinds of logic, and the separation is the product.

Protected — owned by WeQuote

  • Quote lifecycle progression
  • Which quote event derives which Deal stage
  • The meaning of Won, Lost, Archived and Expired
  • Precedence when several quotes disagree

Editable — created by the customer

  • Automations: triggers, conditions, actions
  • Which of their companies each Automation serves
  • Notes, meetings, next actions, labels, interests, watchers
  • File requests and required-work checkpoints

A customer Automation may react to a quote event and organise work around a Deal. It may not redefine what a quote status means, approve a quote, simulate sending or acceptance, or write a protected stage directly.

The protected Phase 1 lifecycle is:

Qualifiedno quote yet
→
In Progressfirst quote created
→
In Reviewconditional
→
Passed Reviewconditional
→
Sentissued to client
→
Won / Lostoutcome

In Review and Passed Review appear only where Quote Review is enabled. Archived and Expired are derived statuses, not pipeline stages.

Why derive rather than let people type

Quote status already drives proposals, invoicing, reporting and the integrations we sell. If a salesperson could set a Deal stage independently, every one of those surfaces would inherit the disagreement. Deriving the pipeline from quotes makes the forecast exactly as trustworthy as the invoice ledger, because it is the same fact read twice.

Roadmap and non-negotiable boundaries

Three phases, ordered by how much structural control the customer gains — because each handover is harder to reverse than the last.

Phase 1 · committedFixed lifecycle
Customer gainsAutomations inside seven fixed stages, from a template or from scratch. Company scope. Draft, test, publish. Required-work checkpoints and file requests.
System guaranteesQuote events are the only thing that moves a protected stage. No custom stages. One owning company per Deal.
Phase 2 · directionCustom stages
Customer gainsTheir own stages inserted into five permitted lifecycle gaps. Forecast dashboards and weighted probability. Migration of existing Deals with impact preview.
System guaranteesProtected milestones stay fixed. Every custom stage receives the identical core library.
Phase 3 · directionFree pipelines
Customer gainsStandalone pipelines for processes unrelated to quoting, with manual or explicitly named stage moves.
System guaranteesNothing — deliberately. These pipelines make no claim about sales truth, so a stage name is only ever a label.

Explicitly out of Phase 1

  • Customer-created custom stages
  • Fully custom pipelines
  • Forecast dashboards, weighted probabilities and forecast-specific filtering or sorting
  • Existing-Deal migration and pipeline-wide structural impact preview
  • Public web-to-lead capture forms Open decision
Why forecasting waits

A weighted forecast built on a Deal model that is still settling produces numbers that will be quoted back at us for years. Phase 2 owns forecast so that Phase 1 can change its mind about the Deal without invalidating a board pack.

Terminology that will cause trouble

The existing Trading Name is not automatically the same entity as the new CRM Owning Company. Trading Name already does two jobs — it decides what appears on an invoice, and it feeds an accounting breakdown. Making it a third thing needs an explicit decision, not an assumption. See §18 · D4.

Two further pieces of vocabulary need holding apart. The existing Sales Overview already groups accepted and complete quotes as "Won" and shows a proposal seen status; the Phase 2 forecast view is a separate thing and this document does not relabel Sales Overview as a CRM forecast. And the existing notes-and-documents feature is not the Phase 1 file-request model, which adds a named request, a required upload, a reminder and a completion state.

Lead

An unqualified opportunity, sitting in the Lead inbox.

  • Created manually, from a form, or by import
  • Creating one creates or links a Customer in the same operation
  • Duplicate matching must let the user attach to an existing Customer
  • Holds title, source, estimated value, owner, customer, contacts, labels, interests, notes
  • Converted or discarded; discarding requires a reason
  • A converted Lead becomes read-only and stays in history

Customer

An organisation or an individual.

  • The Lead flow captures organisation and contact person separately
  • A new customer is flagged prospect until its first Deal, Quote or Project
  • Choosing an existing customer must never create a duplicate

Deal

One commercial opportunity for one customer. The container that owns the pipeline position.

  • Pipeline and stage; owner; one required Owning Company
  • Estimated and derived value; expected close date
  • Risk notes and lost reason
  • Linked contacts with Deal roles
  • Workflow tasks and approvals
  • Scope groups and linked quotes
  • Linked projects and invoices

Quote

A commercial document, linked to a Deal when it takes part in CRM automation.

  • Cannot be attached directly to a Lead
  • May be created from any Deal stage once the Deal exists
  • Quote state and Deal stage remain separate fields

Scope group

A named set of mutually exclusive quote options inside one Deal.

  • One quote may be marked primary in each group
  • Options inside a group do not add together
  • Different groups are additive
  • Accepting one quote accepts its group and system-rejects its siblings
  • Rejected siblings stay in the audit history

Project & Invoice

Downstream records, deliberately not pipeline stages.

  • A Won Deal may prompt the user to create or link a Project
  • An accepted quote may raise an invoice action for Finance
Scope groups are the important idea here

Our customers quote the same job three ways and quote three parts of a job separately, often on the same Deal. A flat list of quotes cannot tell those apart, so any value roll-up guesses. Scope groups make the distinction explicit: within a group the options compete, across groups they accumulate. Everything in §8 depends on this.

A Lead has four states and exactly one forward exit. It never holds a quote.

Creating a Lead

  1. User selects Create Lead.
  2. User searches for an existing organisation or individual customer.
  3. User selects a match, or creates a new prospect customer.
  4. User enters contact and enquiry information.
  5. System creates the Lead and links the customer.
  6. If the New Lead response Automation is on, the system assigns an owner and creates a first-contact activity where one does not already exist.

Converting to a Deal

The order is mandatory and cannot be short-circuited:

Open Leadstep 1
→
Convert to Dealuser confirms
→
Lead read-onlymarked converted
→
Deal createdcustomer already linked
→
Qualifiednever skipped
  • Conversion creates the Deal only — the customer is already linked from Lead creation.
  • Deal title defaults to the Lead title; estimated value and owner carry across.
  • Conversion must never skip Qualified or create a Deal in a later stage.

The UI may offer Convert + Create Quote as one click, but the system still performs two ordered operations: convert the Lead into a Deal in Qualified, then create a draft quote linked to that Deal. The quote begins In Progress; the Deal stays in Qualified until a separate transition occurs.

Conflict · duplicate customer risk

The specification requires a customer record at Lead creation, flagged as a prospect. That means every enquiry — including the ones that go nowhere — creates or matches a customer row. The existing customer list already runs to tens of thousands of records and already needs a dedicated merge tool, which tells us duplicates are a live problem today. See §13 · C9 and §21 for the mitigation this needs.

Deliberate non-goals for Leads

  • No separate Lead kanban board
  • No project created when a Lead is created
  • No quote attached directly to a Lead

The first box is new, and it matters

Company-level visibility does not exist in the product today. Quote visibility currently has two settings: records you own, or records shared with the whole organisation. There is no company, team or territory tier. Because the runtime order above makes company matching load-bearing, this is a new access-control layer and should be estimated as one.

A further consequence: an Automation fires from a queue, not from a person clicking. Access checks in the product today are derived from the signed-in user's session, and a queued job has no session. The account boundary must therefore be resolved and asserted explicitly when the event is received, before any company or stage matching happens.

Which quotes the resolver reads

A Deal may hold several quote families, each with its own revision history. For every family the resolver considers one revision, and ignores the rest.

Relevant quote — inclusion test
Include only ifReason
It is the family's current active revisionSuperseded revisions must not set a stage
It is the family's latest revisionLatest and active are separate flags today and can disagree
It is not a sampleDemo quotes would otherwise derive real Deal stages
It is not a templateTemplate quotes are not commercial work
It is not archivedArchived work is out of play
It is not cancelledCancelled work is not viable
It is not an expired sent quoteExpiry removes viability without any decision being made
Two further flags need a ruling

The quote record also carries an over-quota flag and a sales-order flag. Neither is covered by any current specification. A sales order in particular lives downstream of acceptance, so treating it as a competing quote would fight the Won rule. See §18 · D5.

Order of precedence

Read top to bottom. The first line that matches is the answer.

Aggregate precedence
#If this is trueThe Deal is
1Any relevant quote is accepted or completeWon
2Every related quote is cancelledArchived status
3At least one relevant quote is sentSent
4At least one relevant quote has passed reviewPassed Review
5At least one relevant quote is in reviewIn Review
6At least one quote is a draft or in progressIn Progress
7No quote existsQualified
8Quotes exist, none accepted, none relevant, not all cancelledLost

Line 1 is absolute. Once any quote on the Deal is accepted, the Deal is Won and remaining quotes cannot reopen or downgrade it. Line 8 is the only way a Deal is genuinely lost — by running out of live options, not by someone marking it lost.

Worked example

Hillside cinema roomone Deal · three quotes · one scope group
Option ASentClient opened it twice
Option BExpiredNever opened, lapsed
Option CIn ProgressCheaper alternative, still being built
Deal stage: Sent — engagement 1 / 1 sent proposals viewed. Option B is excluded by expiry; Option C was never sent, so it is not in the denominator.

Sent engagement count

  • Denominator: the latest active, viable, customer-facing sent revision in each quote family.
  • Numerator: those the client has opened at least once.
  • Excluded: drafts, in review, passed review, cancelled, expired, superseded and historical revisions.
  • Detailed view timestamps stay on each quote's own activity timeline.

Backward recalculation

If the quote supplying the highest stage is cancelled or expires, the resolver recalculates from what remains, and the Deal may move to an earlier stage. The old state, new state, source quote and reason are all written to history.

Conflict · this directly contradicts an existing specification

An existing approved document states the opposite: "Never automatically move a Deal backwards", and that an expired quote should keep the Deal in Sent with a follow-up instead. Both behaviours are defensible and they cannot both ship. See §13 · C5 and §18 · D1.

Change orders

Variations on won work are routine for our customers and no current specification covers them. Proposed rule: a change order adds to the Deal's value and appears on its timeline, but does not reopen a won Deal. The Deal shows accepted base value and accepted variation value separately, and change orders stay out of the sent-engagement denominator. Open decision See §18 · D6.

The interface must never present a single blended total. Six different money questions have six different answers.

How value derives

  • With no linked quotes, the Deal uses the manual estimate.
  • With quotes, value derives from the primary quote in each scope group.
  • Mutually exclusive options inside a group never add together.
  • Separate scope groups do add together.
  • A Won Deal locks to accepted amounts; a Lost Deal freezes for lost-value reporting.
  • Billing colours and value order must match the billing progress bar.

Quote events at group level

Group-level effects

Quote cancelledRemove it from active group value and prompt the owner for a next action.
All scope groups droppedRequire Sales to confirm Lost and choose a reason.
Conflict · scope groups versus flat precedence

The precedence ladder in §7 reads quotes as a flat list; the scope-group model above reads them as competing options inside additive groups. These are two different answers to the same question and the consolidated handoff dropped the scope-group model entirely. Scope groups are the richer of the two and match how our customers actually quote. See §13 · C8.

Section 10Phase 1 committed

One block catalogue

One source of truth, not seven libraries. Every stage sees the same core; a stage only adds the quote events that can genuinely happen there.

Shared core — triggers

  • Deal owner changes
  • Next action changes, becomes due, overdue or completed
  • Meeting or site visit changes
  • File added to the Deal
  • Requested file received
  • Deal inactivity reaches a configured duration
  • Expected close date approaching
  • Deal data changes: owner, label, interest, value, company, expected close

Shared core — rules

  • Owner or next action is missing
  • Required task or file is missing
  • Meeting or site visit complete or incomplete
  • Recent activity exists or does not
  • Label, interest, value or company matches
  • Expected close date matches

Shared core — actions

  • Assign Deal owner or a named person
  • Set or clear next action
  • Create note
  • Schedule meeting or site visit
  • Add or remove watcher
  • Add or remove Deal label
  • Add or remove interest
  • Request or attach file
  • Set expected close date
  • Send internal notification

Never in the catalogue

  • Approve a quote or stand in for a reviewer
  • Send a proposal to a client
  • Accept a quote on a client's behalf
  • Mark a Deal Won or Lost
  • Write any protected stage directly
  • Redefine what a stage means

Compatibility invariant

  • All seven stages expose identical core block identifiers, names, settings and validation.
  • A stage may only add quote-specific triggers and rules. It may never remove or rename the shared core.
  • Core actions do not change by stage in Phase 1.
  • Protected progression stays outside the editable catalogue.

Why testing is optional

Forcing a test for every wording change teaches people to click through warnings. Testing becomes mandatory only where validation finds a genuine conflict:

  • An action missing mandatory configuration
  • A referenced target stage that no longer exists
  • A file-received trigger pointing at a deleted file request
  • Circular stage movement
  • A protected milestone used as a generic move target
  • A wait or retry path with no safe ending

Simulator

A test saves a safe snapshot and never touches live CRM data. Timeline is the default tab and supports Play, Step, Reset and Speed. Before-and-after shows the CRM records and fields that would actually change — not two duplicate pipeline columns. Exiting with unsaved work offers Continue editing, Discard changes, and Save draft and exit.

Runtime and audit

  • Only the active published snapshot executes. Drafts, test drafts, inactive and out-of-scope Automations never run.
  • Every run stores the source event, the Automation and version, the company scope, the branch taken and timestamps.
  • Retries are idempotent — no duplicate notes, meetings, next actions, file requests or movements.

Known acceptance defect, and a wider concern

When an Automation creates a scheduled meeting or follow-up for a Qualified Deal, it currently lands only in history — it must appear on the Deal card and in Deal activity as well.

More broadly: the product's existing activity log holds roughly 1,800 entries against roughly 11,000 quotes and tens of thousands of customer records. It is, in practice, unused. The CRM's entire value rests on that timeline, so understanding why nobody writes to it today should shape the interface more than anything else in this document. See §18 · D3.

Two vocabulary rules to hold: Deal labels remain a flexible multi-select — Hot, Warm, Cold, VIP and any customer-created value all use labels, and no separate temperature field is introduced. Interests are a distinct multi-select with their own add and remove actions. Expected close date sits on Deal details beneath labels, and is not a Lead field.

Required-work gate and file requests

When work must happen before a stage can move, the system blocks the move and then resumes the exact operation the user started.

Attemptstage move or quote action
→
Collectevery unfinished item
→
Dialog"have you completed…?"
→

→

→
Resumethe original action
  • List every unfinished item, including where there are ten or more.
  • Mark each item's source — a custom-stage requirement or a quote-lifecycle requirement.
  • File requirements offer Upload file; note, meeting and readiness requirements offer Mark complete.
  • Continue stays disabled until the list is empty.
  • Review in Deal opens Needs your attention.
  • After completion, resume the exact original operation — never substitute a generic fallback.

Requested file is received

This trigger is never created from nothing. It must bind to a real, named, open file request on the Deal.

  • The request may have been created in the current stage, an earlier stage, or manually.
  • Without a valid selectable request, the Automation cannot be saved.
  • Creating a request writes Deal activity, Needs your attention, history, and due-and-assignee status on the Deal card.
  • Uploading the matching file resolves the requirement automatically.
  • Reminder, due-date and assignee changes remain auditable.

Section 15ConflictOpen decision

Existing integrations

We already sell a CRM sync. No source document in this package mentions it.

WeQuote has shipped a substantial two-way Zoho CRM integration for years. It maps every quote to a Zoho deal, and it lets each customer configure how our quote statuses translate into their Zoho pipeline stages — in both directions. It is actively maintained and customers depend on it.

For those customers, our new Deal is a second deal, with a second set of stages. Two systems will hold a position for the same job.

Questions this raises

Which system owns the stage?Ours is derived and read-only with respect to Zoho. We never write our derived stage into their pipeline.

Two smaller consequences. Customer records are already pulled inbound from Zoho, Magento and QuickBooks; those imported customers have no owning company, so a default is needed. And the accepted-quote-to-draft-invoice template must set the invoice to draft explicitly, because the product's default state for a new invoice is not draft.

Section 16Phase 2 direction

Phase 2 — custom stages

Not Phase 1 scope. Documented here so Phase 1 does not build a dead end.

A custom stage may be inserted into one of five lifecycle gaps: Qualified → In Progress, In Progress → In Review, In Review → Passed Review, Passed Review → Sent, and Sent → Won/Lost. Custom stages are not permitted before Qualified or after Won and Lost in a quote-connected pipeline.

Stays identical regardless of placement

  • The complete shared core library
  • Core block identifiers, names, settings, fields and validation
  • Draft, optional test, publish, active and inactive behaviour
  • Runtime and audit semantics

Changes by placement

  • Read-only quote lifecycle context and available runtime data
  • The next protected system event
  • Valid named custom-stage targets
  • Flow summary context
  • Recommended examples and templates — not permissions

Placement is context, not a permission switch. This supersedes an earlier idea that each lifecycle segment has its own core library. Every custom stage saves its own trigger, rules, branches, actions, company scope, active state, draft and test state.

  • Custom-to-custom movement stores an explicit target stage identifier. Relative "move to next stage" is never stored.
  • Custom-to-protected movement happens only through the trusted quote event.
  • One system event moves every Deal in a segment. Adding more custom stages must not create more quote-to-Deal transition rules.
  • If a new stage is inserted between two existing ones, an existing Automation targeting the later stage must not silently change — ask the user which one to target.
  • Structural add, rename, reorder, remove and migration of existing Deals all require an impact preview.

Safe creation flow: duplicate the system template, map the custom stages, review before-and-after, then activate for new Deals only by default. Moving existing Deals requires stage-by-stage mapping, an impacted-record count and administrator confirmation.

A gap list that needs revisiting

There is no permitted gap before Qualified, which means the lead-facing part of the process could never be extended in Phase 2. Since Leads have their own fixed states rather than pipeline stages (§5), the cleaner answer is that Lead states are governed by their own mechanism and no pre-Qualified gap is needed. This should be confirmed rather than assumed.

Section 17Phase 3 direction

Phase 3 — fully custom pipelines

Customer-defined processes that make no claim about sales truth.

  • Every stage is customer-defined. There are no protected quote milestones and no resolver.
  • Every stage exposes the same generic CRM library.
  • Generic events cover stage entry and exit, Deal changes, activity, files and time.
  • Rules cover stage and Deal data, work and evidence, AND/OR groups and safe waits.
  • Actions cover CRM work plus explicit movement to a named stage.
  • Optional quote blocks appear only where related quote data genuinely exists.
  • No automatic movement caused by quote status. No relative next-stage movement.
The rule that makes this safe to build

A stage name is a display label and nothing else. The platform must never infer behaviour from words such as Review, Sent, Closed — or Banana. A stage name never enables or disables a block. All logic references stage identifiers.

Phases 2 and 3 reuse the same generic core. Their only real difference is protected structure and who holds movement authority. When pipeline creation ships, the user chooses quote-connected or fully custom at the start; the system must never implement "remove the quote lifecycle" as a conversion shortcut, and the two types coexist.

Open decision Whether Phase 3 supports templates or only start-from-scratch. The earlier agreed direction was start-from-scratch only.

Section 18Open decision

Open decisions

Eight questions that are not engineering's to answer. The first three change the shape of the product.

D1Do Deals move backwards?

The consolidated model recalculates and lets a Deal step back — a sent proposal expires, and the Deal returns to In Progress because the only live quote is the one still being built. An earlier approved specification states the opposite outright.

RecommendationAllow it. A pipeline that only moves forward fills its Sent column with dead proposals from eight months ago, which nobody trusts and everybody works around in a spreadsheet. Record every step back with its reason, and never step back out of Won.
D2What happens to our Zoho CRM customers?

We already sell a two-way Zoho CRM sync that maps quotes to Zoho deals with configurable stage mapping. Introducing our own Deal gives those customers two pipelines for one job. No source document in this package mentions the integration. Full detail in §15.

RecommendationCoexist, with our stage derived and read-only toward Zoho, and no backward corrections pushed outward. Hold Deals back for Zoho-connected organisations until this is agreed.
D3Why does nobody use the activity timeline we already have?

The existing notes and activity log holds roughly 1,800 entries against roughly 11,000 quotes and tens of thousands of customer records. The CRM's whole value rests on the timeline — the notes, the calls, the reminders, the relationship history.

RecommendationFind out before building. Ask five customers. If the answer is that logging a note takes too many clicks, that finding should drive the interface more than anything else in this document.
D4Is the CRM company the existing Trading Name, or a new entity?

Trading Name already decides what appears on an invoice and feeds an accounting breakdown. Making it the CRM's enrolment scope gives one record a third job. Separately, many existing quotes have no trading name set at all, so a default is needed before a company can be made mandatory.

RecommendationReuse it with the CRM's use clearly flagged, and assign the account's primary company to existing quotes that have none.
D5Which quotes count toward a Deal's stage?

Beyond the seven tests in §7, the quote record carries an over-quota flag and a sales-order flag. A sales order sits downstream of acceptance, so counting it as a competing quote would fight the Won rule.

RecommendationExclude both. Neither should derive a Deal stage.
D6How do change orders affect a won Deal?

Variations on won work are routine and no current specification covers them. A change order can require its own review, send and acceptance cycle.

RecommendationAdd to Deal value, show accepted base and accepted variations separately, never reopen the stage, and keep change orders out of the engagement count.
D7Whose approval threshold applies?

The high-value approval template fires on a quote-value or discount threshold. Those thresholds are configured per user in the product today.

RecommendationThe quote owner's threshold.
D8Remaining smaller rulings

Whether an accepted quote can be reversed, or only reopened with an audit event. Default inactivity thresholds for Leads and Deals. Whether overdue reminders repeat or fire once — the recommendation is fire once with a persistent overdue state. Whether Finance is notified on each acceptance or only once all scope groups resolve. Whether a cancelled scope group changes Deal value before the owner confirms. Whether a Create related quote action is ever exposed. Whether Phase 3 supports templates.

Section 20

Acceptance criteria

Automation builder

  1. A user can open every pipeline shown in Deals from the Automation pipeline chooser.
  2. A pipeline with zero Automations opens to an empty state and Create Automation.
  3. The user first chooses a fixed stage, then template or start from scratch.
  4. Company scope is required and defaults to All Companies.
  5. All seven stages show the same shared core block identifiers and actions.
  6. Only genuinely compatible quote-specific triggers and rules are added per stage.
  7. A customer cannot add a generic action that writes a protected stage.
  8. Saving a draft leaves the live Automation running unchanged.
  9. A test creates no live CRM data, and the timeline supports Play, Step, Reset and Speed.
  10. Publish remains available when a test is missing or outdated, provided validation is clean.
  11. Before-and-after shows actual changed outcomes and suppresses duplicate no-change views.
  12. Runtime ignores drafts, test drafts, inactive and out-of-scope Automations.

Resolver and Deal

  1. The multi-quote resolver follows the declared precedence, including backward recalculation and its audit entry.
  2. All-cancelled becomes Archived; expired remains derived; neither is a stage.
  3. The sent Deal card shows the correct viewed-of-sent denominator.
  4. A sample quote, a template quote and an archived quote never derive a Deal stage.
  5. A family whose latest revision is not the active one derives no stage from that revision.
  6. A post-Won change order updates Deal value without changing the stage.
  7. Accepting one quote in a scope group system-rejects its siblings and they remain visible in audit.
  8. Separate scope groups sum; options inside one group do not.

Lead

  1. Creating a Lead creates or links exactly one customer, and duplicate candidates are always offered.
  2. Converting a Lead creates exactly one Deal, in Qualified.
  3. A converted Lead is read-only and remains in history.
  4. Discarding a Lead requires a reason.
  5. A quote cannot be attached to a Lead by any route.

Runtime and audit

  1. A requested-file-received trigger cannot be saved without a valid named request.
  2. The required-work dialog lists all blockers, keeps Continue disabled and resumes the exact original operation.
  3. An Automation-created meeting or follow-up appears on the Deal card and in activity, as well as history.
  4. Replaying the same event creates no duplicate notes, meetings, next actions or file requests.
  5. An event carrying another account's identifiers is rejected at ingest.
  6. An Automation never executes for a Deal whose owning company is outside the published scope.
  7. No forecast, weighted probability or forecast-specific filter or sort ships in Phase 1.
Automated coverage required

Criteria 13 to 20 and 29 to 31 must be covered by automated tests rather than manual checks. The precedence ladder, the relevant-quote test, backward recalculation, scope-group resolution, idempotency and the account boundary are the least safe logic in the release to leave to a manual pass.
